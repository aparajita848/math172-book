This is an open-source text for students taking MATH 172: Mathematical Modeling for the Life Sciences at University of South Carolina. The topics in this course range from ecological population models to genetics and biomolecular kinetics. 


%What this book entails & how to go about it.
This book encompasses only a few selected topics from the vast scope of mathematical biology; links to further readings and resources have been provided.

## For Instructors
### Order of Chapters

The chapters of this textbook have been sequenced into the following order of sections: 

Simple Continuous Models $\rightarrow$ Discrete Models $\rightarrow$ Multiple-Species Continuous Models $\rightarrow$ Genetic Inheritance.

Although, there are several different variations to the order in which the chapters can be taught. The most logical alternative is to teach Single-Species followed by Multiple-Species models while tackling Continuous & Discrete models separately - and it has been taught in this manner previously. 

This particular order was chosen for the following reason - the majority of the concepts in this course are completely new to the students (who are required to have a minimum calculus background that covers upto integration), and we wish to first make them comfortable with the concept of modeling with differential (or discrete difference) equations by introducing it in the context of several different scenarios. This way, we hope that the student may take away a general understanding of how to interpret & translate a given biological interaction or system into a mathematical equation/s, and be able to further assess and compare it with other available models.

The text merely aims to serve as a guide; the final choice of the order is of course left to the discretion of the instructor.
